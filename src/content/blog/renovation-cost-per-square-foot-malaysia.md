"How much per square foot" is one of the first questions homeowners ask when budgeting a renovation, and it is a reasonable instinct. A single number feels easier to compare across contractors than a full quotation. The problem is that renovation cost per square foot in Malaysia is not a fixed rate the way, say, flooring material is. It is a result you calculate after the scope is fixed, not a number you should be quoting from before it is.

This guide walks through why a psf figure on its own is close to meaningless without knowing what it includes, and shows the actual maths behind it using real ranges from our own [terrace house](/blog/terrace-house-renovation-cost-malaysia/) and [general renovation cost](/blog/home-renovation-cost-malaysia-2025/) guides.

## Why "Per Square Foot" Breaks Down in Renovation

Cost per square foot works reasonably well for new construction, where the building envelope, structure, and finishes are applied fairly evenly across the whole floor area. Renovation does not work that way. A typical project touches some rooms heavily and others not at all, and the areas that get touched are rarely priced evenly.

Take our [terrace house renovation guide](/blog/terrace-house-renovation-cost-malaysia/): a light refresh covering two or three rooms runs RM20,000 to RM80,000, while a full interior renovation across the whole house runs RM80,000 to RM200,000. If you divide either figure by the full floor area of the house, both numbers come out distorted. The light refresh looks artificially cheap per square foot because most of the house was never touched. The full renovation looks more expensive per square foot in the rooms that got the heaviest work, like bathrooms and kitchens, than in bedrooms that mostly got paint and flooring.

## Working Out a Real Cost Per Square Foot

Here is the maths behind a psf figure that actually means something, using a typical single-storey terrace around 22 x 70 ft (about 1,540 sq ft of built-up area), the same reference size used in our terrace cost guide.

| Scope | RM Range | Approx. Cost Per Sq Ft |
|-------|----------|------------------------|
| Full interior renovation (typical 22 x 70 ft terrace) | RM 45,000 - RM 80,000 | RM 29 - RM 52 |

That range only holds because the scope is fixed (full interior, whole house) and the floor area is fixed (the entire built-up area, not just the renovated rooms). Change either side of that equation and the psf figure moves, without the actual work getting any more or less expensive.

A light refresh covering two or three rooms cannot fairly be divided by the whole house's floor area at all, since most of the house is excluded from the scope. The honest way to express that project's psf is to divide its cost by the area of the rooms actually being renovated, not the property's total built-up area. Two contractors quoting the same light refresh could show psf figures that differ by a factor of five simply because one divided by the rooms being worked on and the other divided by the whole house, with identical actual cost.

Extension work breaks the maths further still. A [full renovation with extension](/blog/terrace-house-renovation-cost-malaysia/) adds new floor area that did not exist before the project started, along with structural demolition, new roof and beam work, and in most councils, [authority submission](/blog/authority-submission-renovation-malaysia/) before construction can begin. A psf figure calculated against the finished footprint after the extension understates the real cost per square foot of the work performed, because it spreads structural and submission costs, which do not scale linearly with area, across new floor space that did not require them.

## Why Two "RM X Per Sq Ft" Quotes Rarely Match

When two quotes for what sounds like the same renovation land on very different psf figures, the gap is almost never differing labour efficiency. It is usually one or more of the following:

**Different floor area basis.** One contractor may divide by the property's total built-up area, another by only the rooms being renovated, and a third by usable floor area excluding walls and circulation space. None of these are wrong on their own, but they are not comparable to each other.

**Different scope hidden inside the same total.** As covered in our [general renovation cost guide](/blog/home-renovation-cost-malaysia-2025/), [wet area work](/blog/home-renovation-cost-malaysia-2025/) like bathroom waterproofing and replumbing costs far more per square foot than a bedroom repaint. A quote weighted toward bathroom and kitchen work will always show a higher psf than one weighted toward living areas and bedrooms, even at an identical total budget.

**Material tier baked into the rate.** The same layout can cost RM20,000 or RM80,000 depending on whether cabinetry is laminate or solid wood, and whether countertops are standard quartz or imported stone, exactly as our general cost guide sets out. A low psf figure with no material grade specified is not a bargain, it is an unanswered question.

**Exclusions not stated as exclusions.** A psf figure that looks unusually low compared to the others is worth checking for what has been left out; authority submission fees, waterproofing membrane, or a lower material grade than what was discussed are the most common gaps, as our [guide to comparing renovation quotes](/blog/renovation-quotation-malaysia/) covers in more detail.

## How to Use Cost Per Square Foot Properly

A psf figure is a useful check on a quote you already have in hand, not a shortcut for comparing contractors before you have one. To use it correctly:

1. Confirm exactly what floor area each quote's psf figure is calculated against, ask directly if it is not stated
2. Confirm the psf figure is calculated on the same scope tier, light refresh, full interior, or full renovation with extension, not mixed across tiers
3. Check whether authority submission, waterproofing, and material grade are included in the total the psf figure is derived from
4. Recalculate the psf yourself from the itemised total and the floor area you agree on, rather than accepting a headline figure at face value
